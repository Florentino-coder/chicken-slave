# Lacquer Court

**A visual philosophy for a small, exact set of marks.**

Lacquer Court treats every symbol as a piece of gilt laid on dark lacquer. The ground is deep and quiet, close to the felt of a card table at night. The marks are drawn in one warm gold line of constant weight, so that a crown and a close button feel made by the same hand with the same brush. Nothing is shaded. Depth comes only from a second, translucent wash of the same gold inside closed shapes, as if the gilding had been rubbed thin by use.

Form follows a strict 24-unit grid with a 2-unit safe margin. Strokes are 1.75 units, ends and joins are round, curves are built from as few points as possible. Each icon is reduced until removing one more line would make it unreadable at 16 pixels, and then it is refined again. The work should look meticulously crafted: optical centring corrected by hand, terminal curves balanced, the product of deep expertise rather than a library pulled off a shelf.

Hierarchy is the soul of the set. The five titles of the table, from the highest seat to the lowest, share one silhouette height but lose ornament step by step: points become a band, the band becomes a head, the head becomes a sheaf of grain, and the grain becomes two linked rings. A viewer should feel the descent before reading any label. This progression must be executed with painstaking attention, every step weighed against its neighbours.

Colour is restrained to three values: lacquer black-green for the ground, gold for every line, and a single cinnabar red reserved for the two red suits and for warnings. Red is never decorative. Gold is never mixed with a second metallic. The palette should read as one material, calibrated with master-level care.

Text is a whisper. Labels are small, thin, widely tracked, placed on a strict baseline and never touching a mark. Typography exists only to catalogue, like plates in a field guide of an imaginary craft. Every margin, every gap, every alignment must look like the result of countless hours of refinement by someone at the top of their field.
